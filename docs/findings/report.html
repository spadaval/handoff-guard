Handoff Guard · findings as of

Two in three agent handoffs need work, and nearly all of it is run-together writing

We collected 3,464 messages that coding agents sent each other, had model reviewers read a fair sample of 86 against a written rubric, and ran the guard's mechanical checks on all of them. This page holds the figures and charts. The summary is the short version and the methodology says how the data was collected, cleaned and judged.

Project, mission and thread names are replaced by letters and numbers. The only message text shown is the set of fragments the rubric already quotes, with names, numbers and hashes replaced.

How many handoffs need work

A handoff needs work when its lowest score on intent, content or presentation is 1 (Broken) or 2 (Costly). On the fair sample that is . Messages cluster by mission, so the real uncertainty is wider than the interval.

Intent asks whether the message says what the sender wants. Content asks whether it carries what the recipient needs. Presentation asks whether it can be read in one pass. Senders almost always state the ask. What fails is the writing.

The glued-values check was written by reading the handoffs the other checks missed, which are the same reviewed handoffs it is scored on here. What the clear version costs

Length of the reviewer's plain-English rewrite relative to the text it replaces, 53 rewrites

Reviewers rewrite a handoff when its presentation is Costly or Broken: the whole message when it is 1,500 characters or fewer, otherwise the passage with the main ask. A rewrite may add no facts and must mark what it could not decode as [unclear: …].

Limits

  • Two model judges, no human one. Scores are Claude Opus's, checked on 37 handoffs by Gemini Neon.
  • A narrow corpus. 84% of the handoffs are from November 2026, 88% from one project, and 63% from one mission. Figures describe this corpus, not agent traffic in general.
  • Confounded sources. Sender model, project, month and delivery tool change together. Nothing here says which of them causes the shorthand.
  • No untouched test set. The hook's thresholds were set against this corpus and checked on reviews that include the held-out split. The glued-values check was designed from the reviewed sample itself.
  • Small counts. 86 reviewed handoffs support the headline. Most breakdowns rest on a handful each, and the intervals treat clustered messages as independent.
  • Detection, not effect. Nothing measures whether a recipient acted wrongly on a bad handoff, or what a sender does after the hook blocks one.
